A DICTIONARY OF REGULATORY GENOMICS — STATIC LENS

CDH1's regulatory neighborhood, recovered from co-occurrence alone.

Region2Vec learns each chr16 regulatory region's embedding from the experiments it co-occurs in. We anchor on the CDH1 / E-cadherin promoter (chr16:68,737,071–68,737,417, PLS) and read out its top 30 NPMI partners on two coordinate systems: the learned UMAP embedding (top), and chr16 itself, zoomed three times (bottom). The dictionary surfaces three concentric layers of CDH1 biology — its cis enhancer cluster, the CDH3 paralog 92 kb upstream, and a distal hub at chr16:71.6 Mb — all without ever using genomic distance as input.

What you're looking at. Top: the chr16 region UMAP. Each grey point is one of ~36 k regulatory regions; coloured points are CDH1's top-30 NPMI partners; the black star is CDH1 itself. Bottom: chr16 at three zoom levels — the full chromosome, a 2 Mb window centered on CDH1, and a 20 kb window. Each partner appears in track A and (where it falls within the window) in tracks B and C. Beziers between the UMAP and track A link each partner across the two coordinate systems.

What it shows. Twenty of CDH1's thirty partners are proximal enhancers (pELS) and nine are promoters (PLS). Three of the enhancers sit within 1 kb of CDH1's TSS — the cis regulatory module — and the closest promoter partner is CDH3 (P-cadherin), CDH1's paralog 92 kb upstream. Four more partners cluster ~3 Mb away at chr16:71.6 Mb (a distal regulatory hub the embedding places right next to CDH1). Across the 90 Mb of chr16 the partner distribution is uneven — partners cluster, with long stretches empty — and the embedding mirrors that clustering: PLS partners (including CDH3 and the 15 Mb-distant rank-8 PLS) sit tight against CDH1, while pELS partners scatter more widely. The reading argues that what NPMI captures is functional class plus shared cell-type program, not raw genomic proximity.
